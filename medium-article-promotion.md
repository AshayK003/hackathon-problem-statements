# Stop Building Chatbots: This GitHub Repo Has 46 Problems Worth Your Time

### 46 real-world hackathon problem statements backed by actual datasets, peer-reviewed research, and clear success criteria — so you stop wasting time and start building something that actually matters.

---

I've been there. It's Friday night before a hackathon, and your team is circling the whiteboard for the third hour. Someone suggests "AI for healthcare." Someone else says "maybe something with climate change." Nobody commits. The pizza gets cold. You end up building a chatbot that generates inspirational quotes, and by Sunday nobody cares.

The problem isn't your team. It's the prompts.

Most hackathon problem statements — and I mean 90% of them — fall into three failure modes. They're either so vague you can't start ("Build something with AI for education"), so over-engineered you can't finish ("Build a federated learning platform for genomics in 48 hours"), or so fake nobody believes in them ("Airbnb for pets").

I spent months collecting every hackathon problem list I could find. Blog posts, university pages, GitHub topics, contest archives. And they all had the same flaw: **nobody had actually done the homework.** No datasets linked. No papers cited. No sense of whether the problem could actually be solved in a weekend or needed a year.

Then I found [this repository](https://github.com/AshayK003/hackathon-problem-statements) by AshayK003, and it changed how I think about project selection entirely.

---

## The Real Cost of Bad Problem Statements

Let me be blunt. A bad problem statement wastes more than just your hackathon weekend.

If you're a student, a vague problem means you'll spend half your semester figuring out what to build, leaving no time to implement it well. Your grade suffers, and worse — you miss the chance to produce something portfolio-worthy.

If you're a hackathon organizer, weak prompts produce weak projects. You get ten teams building different variations of the same CRUD app. Sponsors walk away unimpressed.

If you're an indie developer, every hour spent defining the problem is an hour you're not building. And in a weekend hackathon, that's fatal.

The real cost isn't measured in lost time — it's measured in missed *conviction*. When you don't believe in the problem, you won't push through the 2 AM debugging session. You'll cut corners. You'll ship something half-baked.

**A great problem statement makes you want to build. A mediocre one makes you want to sleep.**

## What This Repo Actually Is

It's a curated collection of 46 problem statements organized across 5 tracks. But that undersells it. Every single problem in this repo comes with:

- **Linked datasets and API endpoints** — not "use some data," but "here's exactly where to get it"
- **Peer-reviewed research citations** — 200+ papers across the collection
- **Stakeholder analysis** — quantified: who's affected, at what scale, with sources
- **MVP scope** — build times estimated in weeks or months per problem
- **Adjacent open-source projects** — so you build on shoulders, not from scratch
- **Success criteria** — checklist-style "what done looks like"

The repo is MIT licensed. Fork it, use it, build a company on it. The author explicitly encourages that.

## The 5 Tracks (And Where You Should Start)

### 🌍 Global South Impact — 10 AI/ML Problems
These tackle infrastructure challenges in low-resource settings — maternal health, procurement fraud, informal waste management, crop disease. Build times range from 5 to 18 months. **Best for:** teams with ML expertise and a semester or more of time.

### 🇺🇸 US Civic Tech — 10 Consumer-Facing Problems
This is my favorite track. Every problem here targets a broken consumer system in America — medical billing errors (80% have errors), workers' comp ($50B industry with zero consumer competitors), FOIA requests, predatory lending. Build times: 6–16 weeks. **Best for:** LLM-powered fast iteration. Highest novelty scores.

### 🇮🇳 India Impact — 5 Problems on India's DPI Layer
India's digital public infrastructure is built — APIs exist for land records, court cases, agricultural prices. What's missing is the intelligent application layer. Build times: 8–16 weeks. **Best for:** solo devs comfortable with API orchestration who want startup-level depth.

### 🧠 Frontier AI Platforms — 10 AI Governance & Health Problems
Algorithmic auditing, antimicrobial resistance, clinical trial matching, dementia care. These sit at the frontier where AI capability meets systemic risk. Build times: 2–6 months. **Best for:** teams with domain interest in AI safety, health equity, or climate resilience.

### ⚡ Rapid Prototypes — 11 Pure Engineering Problems
No AI. No computer vision. Just solid engineering — WhatsApp bots, inventory systems, offline forms, data pipelines. Build times: 2–6 weeks. **Best for:** solo developers, weekend hackathons, and anyone who wants to ship fast.

## 3 Problems You Should Look At First

I spent an afternoon reading through all 46. Here are three that grabbed me:

**Workers Compass — Claim Navigator (US Civic Tech, 8–10 weeks, novelty score 10/10)**
The workers' compensation system in the US is a $50B industry with effectively zero consumer-facing software. Claimants navigate a maze of forms, deadlines, and medical documentation alone. The problem statement links to state-level workers' comp data, existing legal aid resources, and maps the competitive landscape (there isn't one). If you want a project with genuine startup potential, start here.

**Village Grain Bank Manager (Rapid Prototype, 2–3 weeks)**
Smallholder farmers across Africa and Asia manage community grain banks on paper ledgers and WhatsApp groups. A single dispute or mismanaged withdrawal can collapse the community's food safety net. This is a 2–3 week WhatsApp bot build. No ML required. No cloud credits needed. Just clean engineering that changes lives.

**Vyavastha — MSME Compliance Copilot (India Impact, 12–16 weeks)**
Indian MSMEs spend ₹13–17 lakh per year on compliance — and they'd happily pay for a cheaper alternative. The problem maps India's DPI APIs (GST, MCA21, DigiLocker, Udyam), links to existing e-governance research, and identifies the exact market gap. This isn't a hackathon project — it's a business plan disguised as a problem statement.

## A Quick Reality Check

This repo isn't perfect. The Global South Impact problems are ambitious — some need 12–18 months and serious domain expertise. If you're a first-year CS student looking for a weekend project, those aren't for you. (The Rapid Prototypes track is.)

And not every problem has been built yet. In fact, none have. The FAQ says "these are open problems waiting for a team to step up." That's both the repo's greatest weakness and its superpower — you're not competing with a dozen prior implementations. You're the pioneer.
